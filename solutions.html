Is it really $0 upfront?

Yes. Keystone funds development, engineering, equipment and construction through our own fund and banking partners. You only pay for the electricity the system produces (PPA) or a fixed lease payment, set below what you pay the utility today.

Who maintains the system?

We do. Keystone owns the system, so monitoring, maintenance, repairs and insurance are our responsibility for the full term. If the system underperforms, that's our risk, not yours.

How big does a project need to be?

We specialize in medium to large projects, roughly 200 kW and up, from a single rooftop to multi-megawatt ground arrays. Our featured projects range from 0.2 MW to 3.9 MW, and our utility-scale work goes well beyond that.

What happens at the end of the agreement?

You typically have options: extend the agreement, purchase the system at fair market value, or have Keystone remove it and restore the site. We set this out clearly in the agreement before you sign.

Will construction disrupt our operations?

We plan around you. Schools are often built over summer break, and treatment plants and factories stay fully operational. Our construction affiliate, KPH Construction Services, oversees every step on site.

How long does it take?

The feasibility review is quick and free. After that, the schedule depends mostly on utility interconnection and local permitting, which we manage for you. Your proposal includes a project timeline, so your board knows what to expect before signing.
